Featured · VnExpress International

My applications to American universities weren't built around a perfect score sheet or an arbitrary checklist of achievements. They were built around a single, coherent narrative deeply rooted in water scarcity in the Mekong Delta, and my relentless drive to engineer a tangible solution.

"During the dry season, some residents travel dozens of kilometers just to fetch clean water, then use it only for drinking and cooking. The sheer exhaustion in their eyes left an indelible mark on me."

That was the opening of my personal statement. I wrote about standing under the scorching sun in Tien Giang, realizing that engineering is meaningless if it doesn't solve real human struggles. This formed the catalyst for Green Rise. Returning home, I was consumed by a single goal: building a physical purification device that could turn brackish water into a lifeline. I traced the arc from my childhood obsession with Lego building, to robotics, and finally to environmental research. Each step became a building block toward a larger purpose.

"I wasn't just assembling circuits and Arduino boards; I was trying to engineer hope for communities that had run dry."

The result of this journey led to admission offers from top-tier engineering programs in the United States, including Vanderbilt (#18 US News), Georgia Tech, UIUC, and UMass Amherst, alongside waitlists at Columbia, Rice, Emory, and Purdue.

Yet, when the time came to decide, I stepped back to evaluate my trajectory. I ultimately chose to attend UTS Sydney. The decision was unorthodox, but driven by absolute clarity. UTS offered an exceptionally strong, forward-thinking Artificial Intelligence program that perfectly aligned with my long-term goals. I wanted to immerse myself in an environment that prioritized cutting-edge AI research and practical innovation, giving me the exact tools I needed to push my technical work to the next level.
